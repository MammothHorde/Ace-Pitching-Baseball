If you're trying to explain baseball closer situations to an AI (for game design, simulation, or content generation), it's helpful to describe them in terms of **inning, score margin, baserunners, outs, and leverage**.

### Simple Definition

> A closer is a relief pitcher brought into the game in the late innings to protect a lead and secure the final outs.

### Common Closer Scenarios

#### Standard Save Situation

* 9th inning
* Team leads by 1–3 runs
* Bases empty
* Goal: record final 3 outs

**AI Prompt Example:**

> "The closer enters in the bottom of the 9th inning with a 2-run lead and no runners on base. The crowd is tense because one mistake could tie the game."

#### High-Pressure Save

* 9th inning
* 1-run lead
* Runners on base
* Heart of the opposing lineup due up

**AI Prompt Example:**

> "The closer enters with a 1-run lead, runners on first and second, one out, and the opponent's best hitter at the plate."

#### Fireman Situation

* 8th inning
* Lead is threatened
* Inherited runners

**AI Prompt Example:**

> "The team's elite reliever enters in the 8th inning with the bases loaded and a 2-run lead. One extra-base hit could change the game."

#### Four-Out Save

* 8th inning, two outs
* Small lead
* Stays through the 9th

**AI Prompt Example:**

> "The closer is called upon for a four-out save, entering with two outs in the 8th and protecting a 2-run lead."

### If You're Building a Baseball Game

You could classify closer situations by "pressure level":

| Pressure  | Situation                                |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Low       | 3-run lead, bases empty                  |
| Medium    | 2-run lead, runner on base               |
| High      | 1-run lead, tying run on base            |
| Extreme   | Bases loaded, tying run on third         |
| Legendary | Tie game in playoffs, season on the line |

### Example AI-Friendly Template

> "Generate a baseball closer scenario. Include: inning (8th or 9th), score difference (1–3 runs), number of outs, runners on base, quality of opposing hitters, crowd intensity, and game importance."

This structure works especially well if you're generating dynamic situations for your pitcher-focused arcade game because it gives the AI clear variables to adjust difficulty and drama.
